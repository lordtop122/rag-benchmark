Artificial intelligence, or AI, is the broad field of computer science concerned with building systems that can perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, perception, and decision-making. Machine learning is one major approach within AI, but the field also includes symbolic approaches, search algorithms, and rule-based expert systems that do not rely on learning from data at all.

Early AI research in the mid-twentieth century focused heavily on symbolic reasoning, where knowledge was represented as explicit logical rules and facts, and a program would apply logical inference to reach conclusions. These systems worked well in narrow, well-defined domains but struggled with the ambiguity and variability of real-world problems, leading to periods of reduced funding and interest known as AI winters.

The resurgence of AI in recent decades has been driven largely by advances in machine learning, particularly deep learning, combined with the availability of much larger datasets and far greater computational power than earlier researchers had access to. Modern AI systems are used in applications such as autonomous vehicles, medical diagnosis support, recommendation systems, and conversational assistants.

A key distinction in AI research is between narrow AI, systems designed to perform one specific task very well, such as playing chess or recognizing speech, and general AI, a hypothetical system with human-like flexibility across many different tasks and domains. All AI systems in practical use today are examples of narrow AI. Questions about the safety, fairness, and societal impact of AI systems have become an increasingly central part of AI research, alongside the traditional focus on improving raw performance.
